# Natural Texture 2.5D

Style ID: `textured-2p5d`

## Visual intent

Explain macro scenes using refined but hierarchical forms, readable interfaces, selective natural textures, and soft lighting. Texture helps identify material/process, distinguishing this style from nearly texture-free color blocks, gradients, and slope shading alone.

## Rendering rules

- **Form:** preserve explanatory relief, thickness, overlaps, cut faces, and edges; allow refined geometry without changing scientific connections.
- **Texture:** concentrate layering, grain, roughness, and flow forms in informative material/process regions. Derive orientation, scale, and physical characteristics from content rather than random noise.
- **Materials:** use curated natural surface features to distinguish media. Texture does not mean gloss or mirror reflections. Preserve boundaries/interior visibility of transparent media when needed.
- **Light:** broad diffuse illumination, moderate contact shadows, coherent shading, and visible structure in dark/highlight areas, without cinematic atmosphere.
- **Color:** use plausible intrinsic colors with coordinated saturation/brightness. Preserve scientific codes without a fixed natural palette.

## Adaptation for complex figures

- Establish detailed key interfaces/processes, quieter secondary scenery, and low-texture text/path regions. Detail follows explanatory value.
- Retain visible informative texture rather than flattening everything to match minimal figures; do not turn the whole image into dense realism either.
- Keep labels/legend flat. Lower local scene contrast or move text slightly rather than hiding material interfaces with heavy opaque cards.
- Detail continuous media/repeated objects only where form/distribution carries science. Use representative treatment elsewhere.
- A style switch adds supported material/form detail without inventing layers, parts, tributaries, or stages.
- During removal, preserve key textures, interfaces, and natural media forms. Restore covered areas continuously from adjacent surfaces without smearing or tracing into dense blotches. Keep scene materials as rasters and rebuild explanations natively in Visio.

## Style prompt

```text
A naturally textured 2.5D scientific scene with refined macroscopic forms, meaningful depth and readable material interfaces. Use selective natural texture and controlled surface detail to explain the specified materials and process regions. Preserve all scientific geometry, relationships and labels. Concentrate texture where it carries information; keep secondary scenery quieter and create low-texture space around annotations and paths. Use broad diffuse light, restrained highlights and coherent soft shading. Retain genuinely useful texture rather than flattening every surface, while avoiding an all-over photorealistic landscape. Show clean cut faces or transparent media only when required. Keep text, pathway arrows and legend marks flat, crisp and stable in their semantic encoding.
```

## Style-specific exclusions

```text
Avoid uniformly dense realism, random decorative grain, noisy particle effects, excessive reflections, harsh cinematic lighting, unreadable shadows, blur, toy/clay materials and heavy cartoon outlines. Do not invent layers, material properties or process stages for visual richness, and do not suppress all informative texture merely to imitate a minimal style.
```

## Visible checks and correction

- Important materials/processes have recognizable texture while secondary regions are quiet. If everything competes, lower local detail without deleting major labels.
- Texture and geometry clarify interfaces without covering paths, layers, or words. If only smooth blocks remain, restore supported material distinctions in key areas.
- Volume/material are rich but restrained; primary scientific relationships are read before realistic scenery.
